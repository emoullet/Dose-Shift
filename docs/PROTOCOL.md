# Medication Timing Protocol

## Objective

Evaluate whether the afternoon drop in vigilance and concentration is related to the temporal profile of antimuscarinic treatment, particularly a possible fesoterodine peak effect, while checking that changing the intake time does not worsen bladder control.

## Reference treatment

- Fesoterodine 8 mg extended release.
- Solifenacin 10 mg.
- Usual schedule: both medications with breakfast, around 08:30–09:00.

## Experimental design

Single-subject A-B-A trial over three weeks, with no dose changes.

### Week 1 — Condition A

Usual treatment schedule: both medications in the morning.

### Week 2 — Condition B

- Solifenacin in the morning.
- Fesoterodine in the evening, ideally around 21:00–22:00.
- This timing change must only be performed after validation by the prescribing clinician or pharmacist.

### Week 3 — Condition A

Return to the usual schedule, with both medications in the morning.

The return to condition A is intended to test whether any improvement seen during week 2 disappears again after returning to the initial schedule.

Do not double a dose to shift the schedule. The transition from morning to evening dosing must be organized so that two doses are not taken too close together.

## Daily measurement schedule

Measurements should be performed around:

- 10:30
- 14:30
- 20:30

At each measurement:

- Sleepiness: score from 0 to 10.
- Mental clarity: score from 0 to 10.
- Concentration: score from 0 to 10.
- Objective vigilance test: short Psychomotor Vigilance Task (PVT), approximately 3 minutes, always using the same device and under conditions that are as similar as possible.

For the PVT, retain primarily:

- Median reaction time.
- Number of very slow responses / lapses, if provided by the test implementation.

## Bladder tracking

At each catheterization, record:

- Time.
- Catheterized volume.
- Whether leakage occurred.
- Any unusually urgent need or atypical bladder sensation.

For the night, also record any leakage or unusual awakening related to the bladder.

## Potential confounders

Each day, briefly record:

- Bedtime and wake time.
- Sleep quality.
- Coffee/tea consumption and timing.
- Alcohol consumption, if any.
- Unusual physical activity.
- Particularly large or unusually timed meals.
- Infection or urinary symptoms.
- Any unusual medication, particularly sedating medication.

## Interpretation

Primary hypothesis: if fesoterodine contributes to the afternoon cognitive drop, moving its intake to the evening should reduce that drop during week 2, and the drop should reappear when returning to morning dosing in week 3.

The analysis should prioritize:

- Change in concentration and mental-clarity scores at 14:30.
- Difference between 14:30 and 20:30 measurements.
- Median PVT reaction time.
- Reproducibility of the change across A, B, and A phases.
- Absence of deterioration in bladder control.

## Precautions

This protocol does not change medication doses.

The fesoterodine timing change must be validated by the prescribing clinician or pharmacist.

If leakage increases, unusual urinary symptoms appear, signs of autonomic dysreflexia occur, or any significant adverse effect develops, stop the experiment and contact the relevant medical team.

This protocol does not replace neuro-urological assessment. In the absence of recent urodynamic testing, urodynamic evaluation remains important for assessing filling pressures, bladder compliance, and the actual effectiveness of treatment for neurogenic bladder.
